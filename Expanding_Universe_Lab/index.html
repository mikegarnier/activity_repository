Expanding Universe Lab — Make-Up Activity

Model the expanding universe with a stretchy string. The dots on the string are galaxies.

Directions

  1. Name your galaxies below. The first galaxy sits on the 0 of the ruler and is always the Milky Way.
  2. Click Lock in names. The names will appear on the string.
  3. Read the ruler and measure the distance from the Milky Way to each galaxy (the center of each dot). Record it in the Distance Before Stretch column. Measure to the nearest 0.1 cm.
  4. Click Stretch. Pay attention to how long the stretch takes!
  5. Measure again and record each distance in the Distance After Stretch column.
  6. Copy your measurements onto your lab paper, then calculate the change in distance and the velocity of each galaxy (see the instructions at the bottom).

4. Calculations (do these on your lab paper)

Change in distance — how far each galaxy moved away from the Milky Way during the stretch:

Change in distance = Distance after stretch − Distance before stretch

Example: a galaxy at 4.0 cm before and 6.5 cm after has a change in distance of 6.5 − 4.0 = 2.5 cm.

Velocity — how fast each galaxy moved away from the Milky Way:

Velocity = Change in distance ÷ Time of the stretch

The stretch took 2 seconds. Example: 2.5 cm ÷ 2 s = 1.25 cm/s. Your velocity units are cm/s.

Think about it: Look at your results. How does a galaxy's starting distance from the Milky Way relate to its velocity?
